How FloodGap models flood risk

FloodGap treats a home's flood risk the way catastrophe modelers and actuaries do: it simulates thousands of possible years, builds a distribution of losses, and prices insurance against it. Every formula and assumption is below. This is a simplified educational model, not an official risk rating.

1. Effective flood depth

deff = dflood − FFE

Water depth that reaches the structure = flood depth above ground minus the first-floor elevation (FFE) you enter. Default FFE = 1 ft.

2. Depth → damage → loss

loss = V · r(deff)

Home value V times a damage ratio r from a FEMA/USACE one-story, no-basement depth-damage curve, linearly interpolated and clamped to [0, 1]. Anchor points: 0 ft → 10%, 1 ft → 20%, 3 ft → 38%, 6 ft → 55%, 12 ft → 80%.

3. Hazard: the stage-frequency curve

d(p) = μ − β · ln(−ln(1 − p))

A Gumbel (extreme-value) curve gives flood depth as a function of annual exceedance probability p. μ and β are fit per FEMA zone from anchor return periods. For example, Zone AE: the 10-year (p = 0.10) flood ≈ 1 ft and the 100-year base flood (p = 0.01) ≈ 4 ft.

4. Monte Carlo expected annual loss

EAL ≈ (1/N) · Σ Li  =  ∫01 L(p) dp

We simulate N = 10,000 synthetic years. Each year we draw a random flood depth from the hazard curve, convert it to a loss, and average. The mean of that distribution is the Expected Annual Loss.

5. Tail risk

VaRα = Qα(L)  ·  CVaRα = E[L | L ≥ VaRα]

Value-at-Risk at α = 0.99 is the 1-in-100-year loss. Conditional VaR is the average loss in years worse than that, the catastrophe tail. These are the same risk measures used in finance.

6. Fair vs. worth-it premium

π = (1/a) · ln E[ea·L]

The fair premium equals the EAL. Under exponential (risk-averse) utility with coefficient a = 1×10−5, the certainty-equivalent premium π is the most a homeowner should rationally pay, and always at least the EAL, which is why buying insurance can be rational even when the premium exceeds the average loss.

7. 30-year value of insuring

NPV = Σt=1..30 (E[Lt] − premium) / (1 + r)t,  E[Lt] = EAL · (1 + g)t

Discount rate r = 3%; climate trend g = 1%/yr nudges expected losses upward over time. A positive NPV means insuring pays off in expectation.
