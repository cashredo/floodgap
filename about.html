FloodGap

A simple tool used to find the cost of a flood near your home.

Check an address

One question we all have:
what would a flood actually cost you?

FloodGap takes three public government datasets that most families never see and turns them into one clear answer about their own home.

Why this exists

When Hurricane Harvey hit Houston, it flooded around 204,000 homes. Most of those families had no flood insurance. Not because they gambled and lost, but because nobody ever told them three things: their home's real flood risk, that regular homeowners insurance never covers floods, and what a flood would actually cost them out of pocket.

Every one of those answers already existed in public government data. It just wasn't in a form a normal person could use. FloodGap fixes that.

How it works

  1. You type an address. OpenStreetMap's geocoder turns it into map coordinates.
  2. We check FEMA's flood maps. The National Flood Hazard Layer tells us the official flood zone at that exact point.
  3. We pull the receipts. OpenFEMA's records show every flood-insurance claim paid in that ZIP code since 1978. Real floods, real dollars.
  4. We do the math. Using simplified FEMA depth-damage curves, we estimate what a typical flood for that zone would cost, subtract any flood coverage, and show the gap.

The math

Each flood zone maps to a typical water depth for its design flood. Depth maps to a damage ratio taken from simplified US Army Corps of Engineers depth to damage curves. Estimated loss is your home value times that ratio; your gap is whatever insurance doesn't cover.

These are honest estimates, not predictions. A real flood can be kinder or crueler than the model. The point is scale: seeing a five-figure gap is what gets a family to price a $600 a year flood policy.

Your privacy

What happens on your device, stays on your device.

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no cookies used for tracking.
  • Addresses you search go directly from your browser to public government and OpenStreetMap services, then the answer comes straight back. FloodGap has no server that sees or stores them.
  • No analytics, no ad scripts, no third party trackers.
  • The only thing stored on your device is your light/dark theme choice.

Data sources

  • FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (flood zones)
  • OpenFEMA NFIP claims dataset (claim counts and payouts)
  • FEMA / USACE depth-damage guidance (loss ratios)
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim (address lookup) and map tiles

FloodGap is an educational tool, not insurance, financial, or engineering advice. Always confirm your official flood zone with FEMA before making insurance decisions.

Who built this

Built by Julian Quevedo, a high school student in Houston, Texas, for the 2026 Congressional App Challenge. I've lived in Houston my whole life, in a city shaped by water. Hurricane Katrina brought many of my neighbors here, and Hurricane Harvey showed all of us how fast an ordinary home can end up underwater. I built FloodGap for families like mine, so no one has to learn their flood risk the hard way.
